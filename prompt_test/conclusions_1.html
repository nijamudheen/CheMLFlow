Reviewer experiment audit · dataset2 / viT.npy · 9 November 2026

What the 30 new agent studies demonstrate

CheMLFlow consistently generated explicit experimental records and repeatedly selected Adaptive NVAR with medium and technical prompts. The runs support consistency of model-family choice and reproducible saved inference, but do not establish an accuracy advantage or identical independently designed studies.

Saved inference versus full study reproducibility

Fresh-process checks passed for all 30 delivered predictors with access to the original dataset and shared source blocked. Each returned finite short-history forecasts, preserved the exact 100-step prefix when asked for 117 steps, left the input unchanged, and reproduced its prediction after an intervening call. Its first hidden-window forecast agreed with the saved evaluation within 10−12 (28/30 bitwise; two differed only at floating-point rounding). These are limited, explicit deployment checks. They are not a full fresh replay of all search/training workflows.

Several native study records also document exact selected-configuration training replay, for example technical repetition 5’s matching weights and window metrics. Those are valuable case-specific checks. A symmetric independent retraining of all 30 complete studies was not performed in this audit.

Errors, restarts and autonomous recovery

All 15 CheMLFlow cases have documented native Torch training crashes, generally return code −11 or SIGSEGV, followed by successful retries with one native CPU thread. Easy repetition 4 had three failed neural configurations in its first pass. Final completion does not erase these earlier failures, and the records distinguish stale “running” status files from processes that remained active.

No substantive failed fit or execution repair was confirmed in the selected standalone sessions under the same adjudication rule. Logs do contain nonzero status probes, optional dependency-metadata checks, inaccessible process-list commands and intentional search interruptions. These are not all training failures. Some standalone MLP trials were deliberately pruned; pruning is recorded search control rather than a whole-experiment restart.

Plain versus technical: did the workflows converge?

At a broad level both conditions profiled the data, fitted candidate forecasters, compared chronological validation errors, selected a predictor, saved preprocessing and fitted state, and verified the inference contract. At implementation level they differ: CheMLFlow uses its native DOE/execution/analysis machinery; standalone cases use project-specific Python/Optuna runners. Formal records and reproducibility evidence are present in both conditions.

Conclusions supported by the evidence

  1. Artifact standardization: CheMLFlow produced generated DOE/configuration/manifest records in all 15 cases, including easy prompts. Standalone agents also preserved extensive model-comparison records, and all five technical standalone cases created the requested DOE YAML.
  2. Model-family consistency: all five CheMLFlow medium and all five technical studies selected Adaptive NVAR. This supports repeatable architecture choice under these prompts, while easy studies selected Adaptive NVAR ensembles in four cases and a local linear AR extension in one.
  3. Protocol consistency: medium and technical CheMLFlow runs shared their respective splits and candidate model type. Technical prompts standardized origins, scoring and final refitting. Both conditions’ technical workflows nevertheless differed in search space, budget and implementation.
  4. Numerical reproducibility: every saved RMSE was reproduced from its prediction archive; all delivered predictors passed the fresh-process checks. This supports deployment repeatability in both conditions. It does not establish identical model discovery or full-study replay.
  5. Accuracy and usage: standalone group means were lower in hidden RMSE100 and experiment tokens at every prompt level. These are descriptive workflow outcomes; different model catalogues, search budgets and medium-group fitting coverage prevent a causal software comparison.
  6. Interaction and errors: all easy studies received clarification replies; medium/technical studies required none in the selected chats. All 15 CheMLFlow cases needed autonomous recovery after native crashes. There is no demonstrated error-reduction advantage here.
Across 30 agent-assisted forecasting studies on viT.npy, CheMLFlow consistently generated explicit DOE and execution artifacts. Medium and technical prompts repeatedly selected its native Adaptive NVAR architecture and produced consistent data splits, while complete designs, hyperparameters and seed policies remained different. Standalone technical agents also created explicit DOE files and converged on analog-based forecasting. Saved inference was repeatable in both conditions. Standalone workflows achieved lower average hidden-test error with fewer logged tokens, subject to unequal search budgets, candidate models and fitting coverage. The results support standardized records and consistent model-family selection with CheMLFlow, but do not establish superior overall reproducibility or accuracy.

Limits that belong in the manuscript

  • One supplied trajectory and one hidden block; five agent studies per group do not establish performance across datasets or noise realizations. Overlapping windows are correlated and are not additional independent repetitions.
  • Conditions were run in blocks: the selected CheMLFlow experiment prompts were sent before the standalone prompts. This was not a randomized or interleaved comparison.
  • Prompt specificity, skill availability and software use are bundled. Installed skills alone are not an isolated treatment. Technical prompts explicitly request DOE files in both conditions.
  • The easy groups received different clarifications, including tuning authorization in four CheMLFlow cases and a balanced-horizon objective in one. They do not test a single unchanged plain prompt.
  • Search budgets, model families, internal aggregation conventions and final fitting coverage differ. The medium comparison especially mixes training cutoffs of 6,000 and 9,000.
  • Local source patches and in-memory extensions changed seed replay, evidence collection or model support. A common package version is not sufficient provenance.
  • The selected 30 experiments all record gpt-6-astra with xhigh effort. These settings differ from the earlier dataset’s recorded experiment settings; cross-dataset differences cannot be attributed solely to the dataset.
  • The three user-designated replacement runs were selected retrospectively. Their predecessors’ costs are disclosed separately; their replacement reasons are not inferred.
  • Original logs may contain truncated tool output, approval clicks are not fully observable, and final manifests may omit overwritten historical attempts. Error/intervention counts are confirmed lower bounds.
  • No symmetric independent replay of all 30 complete model searches and training workflows was performed. Fresh inference checks cover specified histories and horizons, not every possible input.
